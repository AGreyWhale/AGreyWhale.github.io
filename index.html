About

Where engineering, research, and real people meet

Hi! I'm Gauri, a Computer Science student at Texas A&M University with minors in Mathematics and Game Development & Design.

Recent highlight: Outside of research, I'm always building something. I enjoy turning ideas into polished applications, experimenting with new technologies, and refining products until they feel intuitive and enjoyable to use. Lately, that has meant creating PaperTrail, an AI-powered research paper assistant designed to make reading and organizing academic literature feel less like using a chatbot and more like using a thoughtfully crafted reading app.

I enjoy building software that solves interesting problems, whether that's designing a full-stack web application from scratch, developing machine learning models for scientific research, or exploring how AI can make complex information more accessible. Over the past few years, I've worked on everything from modern web applications and distributed systems to computer architecture and machine learning research. Along the way, I've realized that what excites me most is combining strong engineering with thoughtful design to create tools people genuinely enjoy using.

My path into machine learning wasn't a straight line. I spent time working in virtual reality research before discovering that I was most drawn to the data, algorithms, and engineering behind intelligent systems. Today, I'm a researcher in Texas A&M's DIVE Lab, where I develop machine learning models for scientific computing, including predicting complex physical phenomena from simulation data. At the same time, I love building production-quality software, which is why many of my personal projects focus on full-stack development, backend architecture, and developer experience.

I also love talking to people. Whether you want to discuss machine learning, software engineering, research, career advice, or just share something you're excited about, I'd be happy to chat. Some of the best opportunities I've had started with simple conversations, and I'm always interested in meeting people who enjoy building, learning, and solving interesting problems.

When I'm away from my computer, you'll probably find me exploring new coffee shops, taking photos, playing games with friends, or planning my next project before I've finished the current one.

  • Designed and implemented a two-stage deep learning framework to emulate quasi-classical trajectory (QCT) simulations for N + N₂ reactive collisions, reducing reliance on computationally expensive physics simulations.
  • Developed a residual MLP to predict dissociation probability and product-state distributions from molecular initial conditions, using multi-task learning with binary cross-entropy and KL divergence losses.
  • Built a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) in PyTorch to generate full three-atom collision trajectories conditioned on molecular states, reaction outcomes, and quantum mechanical features.
  • Implemented and evaluated conditioning strategies, including explicit (E, v, J) quantum-state features and FiLM-based conditioning, performing ablation studies and out-of-distribution generalization experiments across energy levels.
  • Created scalable training and evaluation pipelines for a dataset of 5.5 million+ simulated trajectories, leveraging SLURM, GPU acceleration, automated evaluation scripts, and visualization tools for model validation.
  • Analyzed generated trajectories using physics-informed metrics, including bond-length reconstruction, KL divergence, and differential cross-section estimation, to validate model performance against ground-truth QCT simulations.
